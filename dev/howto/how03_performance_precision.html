How to Load an Audio-to-Score Alignment Corpus and Compare Performances

A Performance Precision specimen bundles one symbolic score with several recorded performances of it, plus the audio-to-score alignments that connect them. This guide loads such a specimen in a single call and arrives at the question the corpus was built to answer: how do different pianists shape the same piece in time?

The specimen here is Chopin’s Nocturne in E♭ major, Op. 9 No. 2 — a .solo score, a Verovio timemap giving each measure’s position in quarter notes, and an Alignments/ directory holding three alignment files (note, bar, beat) for each of seven recordings. By the end we will read, for one shared score position, the seven different moments at which each pianist played it.

The arc:

  1. Load the whole specimen in one call.
  2. Inspect the measure structure recovered from the timemap.
  3. Read the score (in quarters) and see how a measure+offset label resolves.
  4. Read the per-performer s (in seconds).
  5. Inspect the s, tagged by granularity.
  6. Compare the performers’ timing through the .

2. The measure structure

The Verovio timemap records each measure’s absolute position in quarter notes. The loader walks it into a — a from a quarter position to its measure count — and exposes it directly:

metric_map = loader.metric_map
metric_map
MetricMap(n_measures=38, total_length=425/2)

Thirty-eight measures spanning 212.5 quarters. The companion .meter file is deliberately left unread: it encodes only meter changes (four rows across the whole piece), so it cannot by itself bound the final measure. The timemap’s terminal position supplies that bound, which is why it is preferred here.

The paired is a carrying both directions of the measure↔︎quarter relationship. quarters_at(mc, beat) goes from a metrical position to a quarter coordinate; mn_at(quarters) returns the measure-number label at a quarter position:

3. The score timeline

The score is a logical measured in quarters. It holds every note of the .solo score, each placed at its absolute quarter position. The Projections the alignment’s note claims stand on are not here yet: they are added when the bundle is created (§5). The loader asks for it by its role, "score"; its stored ID is the role followed by a minted one, score:clt_…, seeded from the specimen’s files.

score_tl = loader.create_timeline(uid="score")
score_tl

How a measure+offset label becomes a quarter coordinate

Both the .solo score and the alignment files write score positions as "<measure>+<offset>", where the offset is given in whole notes. The resolver is one line of arithmetic — measure_start + offset × 4 (the × 4 converts whole notes to quarters) — with one wrinkle: measure 0 is the anacrusis (the pickup), whose offsets are measured back from a virtual full-bar downbeat preceding the first sounding note. The very first note of the piece, labelled 0+11/8, therefore lands exactly on quarter 0:

first_three = score_tl.get_events().table.slice(0, 3).to_pandas()

A note on pitch

The score timeline carries pitch as the raw MIDI pitch integer that the .solo file recorded — faithful to the source, which notes no accidental spelling. When a typed pitch field is wanted, it lives on a freshly composed SoloLoader reading the same .solo file. There the field materialises as EnharmonicPitch scalars, and the canonical glyphs ♯/♭ render in their labels:

solo = SoloLoader.from_file(SOLO_FILE)
pitch_field = solo.events.get_field(EnharmonicPitch)
{"first five pitches": [pitch_field[i] for i in range(5)]}
{'first five pitches': [EP(A♯/B♭4),
  EP(A♯/B♭4),
  EP(D♯/E♭2),
  EP(G5),
  EP(D♯/E♭2)]}

Because .solo records only the MIDI pitch number, an enharmonic pair such as G♯3 and A♭3 both surface as the same number — the field reports what was represented, not what could be inferred.

4. The performance timelines

Each recording is a physical measured in seconds, one Note per aligned note onset, and it gains no Projection with the bundle (§5). Each is named by its performer key, by which the loader also finds it; its stored ID is minted, perf:cpt_…. The Ashkenazy recording is "Chopin_Ashkenazy":

A score position is in quarters; a performance position is in seconds. The alignment is what relates the two, and that is carried by the s.

A MatchClaim joins two events. An alignment row names its score position by a measure+offset label, which may stand for several notes of a chord, so on a note row the score side is a : an event added to the score timeline at the label’s position when create_bundle() states the claims, naming the performer’s Note it stands for (projection_of). The score shown in §3 held Notes only and holds these Projections from here on. An unmatched note row is a NOMATCH stated from its score position, so its score side is a Projection of no event, one per position. A bar or beat row has no note on either side: both sides are the sentinel CHUNK, whose coordinates the claim’s anchor states, and no Projection is added for them, so the recordings hold none:

Reading that across a sequence of downbeats gives each performer’s arrival time at each measure — the raw material of a tempo comparison. We take the first several measure downbeats, ask the bundle for each performer’s onset there, and tabulate the seconds against the score quarters:
