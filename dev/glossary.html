Glossary

Alphabetical reference of TimeToAlign! model terminology

This page collects all terms introduced in the Conceptual Model. Hover over any -highlighted term elsewhere on this site to see its definition.

For the full context in which each term is introduced, follow the cross-references to the relevant section of the conceptual model.

A

Accolade
One line of a page of music (a system), read from left to right: one horizontal piece of the page’s , and the along it. An accolade is as long as its piece, states the area of the image it stands for in meta["box"] and the image in meta["image"], and holds the es of the areas lying on it. A page is the of its accolades, a volume the segment line of its pages; neither states an area. TimeAxisPath.from_boxes() finds the accolades among the areas of a page given in reading order: a box begins a new accolade when its left edge lies left of the previous box’s by more than half the span of all left edges (group_into_accolades). See How to Represent Areas of Page Images as Boxes.
AlignmentAnchor
A coordinate pair associating one coordinate on timeline A with one on timeline B; a neutral record with no claim semantics—provenance is carried by the enclosing . Only synchronous MatchClaims produce AlignmentAnchors. From a of s, one derives a StartAnchor and an EndAnchor. See Alignment.
AlignmentBundle
A collection of s with inter-group connections; provides coordinate transfer via offset arithmetic, interpolation, and s. See Alignment.
AlignmentMatrix
An audio-to-audio alignment in which several recordings of one work are each warped onto a single shared, equidistant reference grid. Per recording, a parallel onset-time array gives that recording’s clock-time at each grid column; because every array indexes the same grid they are equal length, so together they form a dense matrix of shape (recordings × grid columns). A single such file encodes an entire directly — every pair of recordings is related at every grid column — rather than only one score-to-performance correspondence. The complete pairwise claim set is held in a . See Alignment.
Archive member
A file inside a ZIP archive, named by the standard library’s zipfile.Path (the archive and the member’s path inside it). Every loader reads an archive member wherever it reads a path, without extracting the archive; a loader whose parser needs a file on disk reads the copy materialize() makes once in a cache. A string is always a filesystem path, never a member. A loader’s metadata["sources"] records a member as the archive’s absolute path plus member, its path inside the archive. See How to Load Data.
Atomic section
A stretch of a ’s axis that its flow control never divides: an interval between consecutive coordinates of its s, s, s and s and the places their s go on at, named by a letter (A, B, …, Z, AA, …) in the order of the axis. Every flow is a sequence of atomic sections. Timeline.create_flow_maps_from_flow_control() writes them as s stating the type tts:AtomicSection, and the computed s list them. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.

B

BeatGrid
An ordered set of BeatGridSegments and the beats they generate on a seconds axis. Each segment states one stretch of a recording — an anchor instant, a tempo, a , and which beat of a bar the anchor is — and generates beats without end; the grid orders the segments, bounds each by its successor’s start and the last by its extent, and labels the resulting beats with measure and beat numbers. Each labelled beat is a GridBeat. A BeatGrid is not a and stores nothing per beat. See How to Build Beat Grids.
BeatPolicy
How one bar is counted in beats: a grouping cycle over one counted note value. A 4/4 bar is four beats of a quarter, a compound 6/8 bar two beats of three eighths, an uneven (3+2+3)/8 bar three beats of unequal length. Beat lengths are derived from the grouping and the division, never stored. One signature admits more than one counting: a ’s lattice reads 6/8 as six beats of an eighth, because its beat-in-bar index counts the notated value rather than the felt pulse.
BoundingBox
An axis-aligned area of an image: x, y, width and height (the xywh of IIIF and Media Fragments), in image space (origin the image’s top-left corner, x right, y down), all four in one unit and one number type; width and height are never negative. A shape, as is : built with from_xywh(), from_corners() (MEI’s ulx, uly, lrx, lry) or from_media_fragment(), and written with to_dict(), as_media_fragment(), as_svg_points() and as_mei_zone(). Its numbers are never rounded: a value the axis cannot hold exactly (385.5 on pixels) is refused. Every shape has a bounding box, the smallest BoundingBox holding it. See How to Represent Areas of Page Images as Boxes.
Box
An area of an image as an interval event of a graphical running along a horizontal piece of the image’s , such as an of a page. A Box holds exactly one shape, a or a , in the image’s space, and the interval of the shape’s x extent on its timeline: start is the shape’s x_min less the x at which the timeline stands in its image (the x of its meta["box"], 0 where it states none), duration is x_max − x_min. Box.from_shape(shape, origin=, id=) makes it; adding a Box whose interval contradicts its shape, or whose shape is on another unit or number type than the timeline, is refused on every path that adds events, a package read included. DmosLoader and MeiFacsimileLoader read Boxes onto the accolades of their pages and name them with box_ids; an ’s to_file() writes every timeline’s Boxes beside the manifest in one table, boxes.parquet, named in its resources with the type Box (see ), and from_file() reads them back. See How to Represent Areas of Page Images as Boxes.
Barline
A recording a barline the source prints (a double or a final barline). It changes nothing in the flow. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.
Break
A at which the flow does not go on: when the flow plays up to it, a at the same coordinate with a that holds is taken, otherwise the flow ends there and begins again at the next , if any. With ends (a condition: passes, after) it ends the flow only while that condition holds, as a Fine does after a D.C. or D.S. A Break lies between two brackets of alternative endings. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.

C

Catalogue
Data shipped with the TimeToShare package that guides the writing of s and that tools read to offer properties and values and to print pairs in words: the prefixes of the vocabularies its patterns use, from which a written manifest’s inline @context takes those its statements use; aliases, namespaces read as equal (https://schema.org/ as http://schema.org/); the identifier systems (Wikidata, MusicBrainz, the GND, VIAF, MeLODy with LRMoo, IMSLP and others, and the fallback vocabularies that state a relation where a system has no property of its own); the verbs, relations in words (is_a, performed_by, part_of, …) each with a pattern per system; and the types of thing a subject or value is. The validators never consult it. add_verb() states a verb in one system’s vocabulary, and get_rectangles() gathers the pairs it reads as one verb about one thing. See How to Describe What a Bundle and Its Timelines Stand For and TimeToShare’s description of a manifest, section 10.
ChainMap
A that applies multiple s in sequence, creating a conversion path from a source unit to a target unit. See Conversion maps.
Child
A nested within a parent, sharing its measuring unit but defining its own origin. add_child() locks a child by default, which prevents side effects from modifications. An unlocked child’s length may change only within its parent’s known length: a parent never grows because a child grew. A child holds its parent’s positions from its start up to its end, and its end only where no sibling starts there; a nested timeline is reached only through parents that all hold the position. A child created with measures=True holds the slice of its parent’s measure map over its span; by default it holds none. See Nested timelines.
CHUNK
A sentinel value in a synchronous for a side whose coordinate the claim states without an event: the claim’s s hold the coordinate (its start, and its end for a span), and no event or is added. A synchronous claim between two bare positions is CHUNK on both sides; MatchClaim.from_events() also takes a position on one side only. The MatchClaim table writes it as CHUNK_SENTINEL in the side’s event ID column; in code it is the constant CHUNK, and MatchClaim.chunk_sides lists the timelines of a claim’s CHUNK sides. Unrelated to the chunks of a , the stretches it plays. See Alignment Bundles and MatchClaims.
ClaimType
The semantic kind of a , derived from its structure rather than stored — read from whether the claim is synchronous and whether both of its sides name an event or one names the sentinel. Each side of a MatchClaim names an event or a sentinel: a side without an event of its own is given a , except on a synchronous claim between two bare positions, which is on both sides. Three members: event_match (synchronous, an event or CHUNK on each side; the case that yields s), nomatch (non-synchronous, one event and the sentinel on the other side; see ), and conceptual (non-synchronous, an event on each side — a structural correspondence with no temporal commitment). What a infers through groups is the graph’s own inferred edge and has no kind here. See Alignment.
CombinationMap
A yielding outputs from multiple s simultaneously (e.g., (x, y) coordinate pairs). See Conversion maps.
Commensurability
Two timelines are commensurable if connected by a chain of s, membership in the same , or cross-group s, enabling coordinate translation between them. See Alignment.
Commensurable
See .
Complete form
The form in which to_dict(minimal=False) writes a : every key the format defines is written, those holding their default value included (null, {}, []). A key whose default is its absence, such as a manifest’s id or a root timeline’s offset, is written only when it has a value. The complete and the of one object read back to equal objects, and each writes back to itself. See How to Save and Load Timelines, Groups and Bundles.
ConcatenationMap
A combining bounded s such that each coordinate region is handled by a specific map. See Conversion maps.
Contiguity
A is contiguous if it monotonically spans all coordinates between its start and end. An is contiguous with a TimeInterval if it is synchronous with its . See Flow control.
ConversionMap
A typed function attached to a that maps each coordinate to exactly one value (coordinate, label, or filename); also permits inverse conversion back to the timeline’s own unit. Also called C-map. See Conversion maps.
Coordinate
A position on a , expressed as the distance from the origin in the timeline’s measuring unit. Combines a numeric value with a TimeUnit. The unit alone decides the number policy: quarters and beats are exact Fractions, discrete units (ticks, samples, frames, pixels) are locked to int, and the remaining continuous units (seconds, points, centimeters, …) are float. That declared number type is preserved at every boundary — authored, converted, offset-computed, interpolated, queried or retrieved — so a fraction-canonical axis always yields Fractions and a float-canonical axis always yields floats. An explicit number_type= overrides the default, and exact input is never degraded. See Foundations.

D

Destination
One place a may go on at, and when: a JumpDestination. Its place is to (the of a , a , a child timeline or the timeline itself), by (a signed distance from the Jump; by: 0 is going on and is never taken) or neither (the start of its section). Its conditions all must hold: passes (the arrivals at the Jump that count), repeat_count (taken at most that often), after (another Jump’s destination must have been taken) and determined (false for a choice nobody can check, such as an ossia, which is never taken). reset says whether going back restores the Jumps and Breaks gone back over, as a repeat does; a D.C. or D.S. played without repeats states reset: false. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.
DictStore
A generic EventStore subclass holding a dict[str, EventData] with arbitrary keys. Used internally by AlignmentLoaders to store one EventData table per timeline-to-be (e.g., "score:clt_…", "perf:Chopin_op10_no3_p01:cpt_…"). Maintains the uniform EventStore interface. See the API reference.
Discrete
A timeline is discrete when coordinates exist only at discrete points (e.g., pixels, samples, ticks). Contrast with Continuous timelines where coordinates can take any real value (e.g., seconds, quarters). See Foundations.
Domain
One of three temporal categories: Graphical (visual/spatial), Logical (symbolic/musical), or Physical (audio/sound). Each domain has both discrete and continuous timeline variants. See Foundations.

E

element id
The id that identifies an element of a manifest: the manifest’s own, a (root or descendant), , MeasureMap, at any nesting depth, , , , or . It is unique across the whole manifest and every kind, so the id alone names the element. An id is free: whatever was given is kept verbatim, any non-empty string without a control character; spaces, colons, slashes and URIs are allowed, and no word is reserved. Only an element given no id gets a minted one, when it is constructed: a prefix, _ and 13 random base-36 characters (region_3kq9x0m2ztb7a). The characters are seeded, so a run repeats them: a loader seeds from the bytes it reads, an object built in code from its content, and a seed= argument makes them exact. The prefix names the kind: clt, dlt, cpt, dpt, cgt or dgt for a timeline (continuous or discrete, logical, physical or graphical), group for a group, measure_map for a MeasureMap, cmap for a conversion map, region for a Region, resource for a Resource, jump, jump_target, break, section_break or barline for a flow-control element and flow for a FlowMap; a virtual timeline is minted as a timeline of its unit; a loader role leads it (score:clt_…). An id is never renamed: when trees are joined into a group or bundle, an id held twice is refused, minted or given alike, for every kind of element, before anything changes. The id identifies, the name displays: a name may be absent or repeat, and an id is never derived from it.
EndInstant
An marking the end of a . Its coordinate must be greater than or equal to the corresponding ’s coordinate. See Foundations.
Event
Anything associated with a via s. An has zero duration; a has duration defined by start and end coordinates. See Foundations.

F

Finding
What playing flow control found, without raising: a flow that never ends (endless_loop), a flow ending at a that states no condition before the end (flow_ends_at_break), two s of one Jump holding at once (ambiguous_targets), a destination nobody can check (undetermined_target), and a stretch never played (never_played). The default and single flows have findings, the printed flow none; Timeline.get_flow(convention) returns them with the flow. A finding may name its cause (FlowFinding.cause, a FlowFindingCause): a stretch the single flow leaves out that the default flow plays has the cause single_playback (going through once takes the last volta ending). See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.
Flow control element
One of the elements a holds as its flow control (a manifest’s flow_control array): a , a , a , a or a . Each lies at one coordinate and has an ; how it looks in the source is its manifestation, which decides nothing. An element is never changed or deleted to repair flow control: an actor’s belief "false" rejects it (flow_control.with_belief()), and the right one is added. Timeline.get_flow() plays the elements no actor rejects. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.
FlowMap
One order in which stretches of one root are played: an ordered list of chunks, each the ID of a , a child timeline or a of that timeline’s tree, or a Gap in which nothing of the timeline is played. A FlowMap holds no interval; resolved against its timeline (resolve()) it places each stretch where the one before it ends and converts positions both ways: unfold() takes a position of the timeline to every position it is played at, fold() a position of the flow to the position it plays, or None in a gap. Timeline.create_flow_map(chunks) creates and attaches one; Timeline.create_flow_maps_from_flow_control() writes the flows of the flow control (default, single, printed) as FlowMaps over the s. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.

G

I

IdCoordinate
A that also names the it belongs to. It is the default return of every coordinate getter in the library — stamps, timelines, groups, bundles, match lines, graphs and warp maps all hand back IdCoordinate(value, unit, timeline_id) unless an explicit format= asks for something else. Carrying identity is what lets a retrieved coordinate be handed straight back as a query without restating which axis it came from. An IdCoordinate never compares equal to a plain Coordinate in either direction; use format="coordinate" when timeline identity should be discarded. See Foundations.
Instant
Associates a (a “time-point”) with a signification such as “start of event e”. Instants sharing a coordinate are synchronous. See Foundations.
InstantEvent
An defined by a single , having zero duration. See Foundations.
Interval
A frozen scalar pairing two plain s as a half-open span [start, end): the start is inclusive, the end exclusive. Both endpoints must share one TimeUnit and one number type, which makes the interval’s own unit and number_type unambiguous; its duration is exactly end - start, returned as a Duration. Reversed intervals raise; zero-length ones are valid. It renders through the same formatter the scalars use, so an interval on a quarters axis prints [1/2, 3/2) quarters rather than [0.5, 1.5). Interval is the scalar leaf of interval retrieval: TimeIntervalStamp.get_interval() and TimelineGroup.get_interval_for() both return one. See Nested timelines.
IntervalField
The SemanticField paired with . It stores a struct of start and end coordinate structs under one unit, domain, and number_type metadata triple, materialises Interval | None per row, and exposes vectorized start, end, and duration accessors returning CoordinateField, CoordinateField, and DurationField. See Nested timelines.
InverseMap
The reverse transformation of a bijective . See Conversion maps.

J

Jump
A where the flow may leave the axis, holding one for every place it may go on at: a repeat end, a D.C., a D.S., a To Coda and the brackets of alternative endings at one coordinate are destinations of one Jump. When the flow plays up to it, of the destinations that hold one is taken (one going back with reset first, then one going back without, then one ahead, the nearest first); when none holds, the flow goes on. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.
JumpTarget
A marking a place a names by its : a repeat start, a segno, a coda, a bracket. Nothing happens when the flow plays up to it. See How to Encode and Play Flow Control.

L

Length
The distance between a ’s origin and its last , or None when it is not known (see ). See Foundations.
Locked
A timeline state that fixes its coordinate axis: no s may be added after the last time-point, so its length cannot change. Locking governs the coordinate axis only — non-structural annotations such as s may still be added to a locked timeline. A timeline is locked with locked=True, when add_child() embeds it as a with its default locked=True, or after construction with lock(); a child read from a manifest keeps the manifest’s locked. A lock cannot be lifted. A locked timeline’s length may still be unknown. On a locked timeline, setting length, append_child() and append_segment() raise RuntimeError, and content, a child or a MatchClaim position (AlignmentBundle.create_match_claims()) past its end is refused with a ValueError before anything is added, unless add_events() or add_child() is given allow_expansion=True; create_child_from_region() gives a child of any region within its length. A loader locks a timeline whose source states its length: AudioLoader a recording at the file’s length, a score loader a score whose measures all state their length, at the end of its last measure or of its last event, whichever is later. Containment does not depend on the lock: a child, locked or not, ends within its parent’s known length, and content or a child added into an unlocked timeline grows that timeline only. See Nested timelines.

M

Match
See .
MatchfileLoader
Loader for Vienna Match (.match) alignment files. Uses the standard two-step load() → create_*() pattern. A single instance processes all .match files that share the same score, building a shared score timeline on the first file and verifying subsequent files against it. Returns an directly via create_bundle(). Score goes into its own group; performances are standalone timelines; s connect score to performances. See the API reference.
MatchClaim
A claim of equivalence between two events on different timelines, each coordinate on its own timeline’s axis, with its (how it was made and who believes it) and a certainty. A side without an event of its own is given a , or is the sentinel on a synchronous claim stated from positions. May be synchronous (producing s) or not: conceptual (structural equivalence without temporal commitment) or (an event on one side, the sentinel on the other). A MatchClaim is always stated: what a infers through groups is the graph’s own (its inferred edges), never a MatchClaim. A person stating claims is recorded as believing them "true" (create_match_claims(..., actor=)). A claim is never deleted: who disagrees records a belief "false" in it (AlignmentBundle.add_match_claim_belief()), and every reader of claims, the MatchStamp and coordinate getters included, takes the belief filter (believer=, beliefs=; see ). Readers of claims take the same timeline filters: timeline_ids keeps a claim with any of the named timelines on either side, within a claim whose both timelines are in the set. Held individually, or in the bundle’s one MatchClaim table, a . Its ID never changes: a claim given none is minted one when it is constructed (claim_ and 13 random characters, seeded from the claim’s content or by seed=), and joining an mints nothing. A MatchClaim is addressed by its ID alone: a bundle or field already holding an ID refuses a second claim under it, and merging bundles (from_bundles()) refuses, before anything is merged, two sources holding one ID. See Alignment.
MatchClaimField
The MatchClaim table: a columnar, PyArrow-backed store holding a set of s of every kind as Arrow columns, one per property of a MatchClaim (the two timeline IDs, the start and end anchors, each holding coordinate_a and coordinate_b only, the row’s timelines being theirs, is_synchronous, the provenance and certainty, the event IDs, each an event’s ID or a sentinel’s string and never null, the event names, and id), rather than as one frozen MatchClaim object per claim; its columns are TimeToShare’s MatchClaim table. Each anchor coordinate is a cell naming its own unit and number_type beside its number. In memory (table) the number keeps both sides, value and the exact numerator / denominator; the package form (package_table, the table to_parquet() writes) writes it once, in the member its number type names (an int as numerator alone, a fraction as numerator and denominator, a float as value alone), the others null, and from_package_table() reads that form back, refusing a cell that does not stand as its number type says and a null coordinate. A fraction is read in any terms (2/4 is the value 1/2), and a claim gives an end on both sides or on neither. Either way a claim’s coordinate comes back in its axis’s declared number type instead of being flattened on the way through the column. Individual MatchClaims are materialised on demand when a row is indexed or iterated. Built vectorized via from_columns(), it is the scalable backing for dense alignments, where the complete pairwise topology of a whole work runs to roughly a million claims, and an keeps all its claims in one, match_claim_table. Shared provenance is held once at field level; only claims whose provenance differs carry it per row. The table property gives the field as an Arrow table, the shared provenance in its schema metadata under the key timetoshare:provenance, and from_table() reads it back unchanged; to_parquet() and from_parquet() write and read match_claims.parquet in the package form. An ’s to_file() writes its table there, beside the manifest, named in the manifest’s resources (see ), and from_file() reads it back; writing over an existing table keeps every claim it holds (the succession rule: same timelines, events and anchors, only the provenance may differ). to_dict() renders the same table as {"provenance": …, "rows": […]}, one mapping per claim keyed by the columns, and from_dict() reads it back. See Alignment and How to Keep MatchClaims, Projections and Beliefs in a Package.
MatchGraph
An auxiliary structure formed by extending s through shared coordinates; may be viewed as a hyperedge connecting multiple coordinates from different timelines. Connected components yield s. Its edges are the anchors of the synchronous MatchClaims it was given (stated) and those extend_to_groups() infers through membership (inferred_edges, each naming its group and the claim it comes from); an inferred edge is the graph’s own, never a MatchClaim. filter() keeps the claims the unified filters choose (timeline_id and timeline_ids on either side, within both sides in the set, the belief filter, stated_only for no inferred edges) and rebuilds the graph from them, extended through the same groups. Each Match box M1–M15 in the Hendrix figure is a MatchGraph. See Alignment.
MatchLine
An ordered sequence of s derived from s, sorted by coordinate on a source timeline; input for generation. See Alignment.
MatchPath
A path traversal connecting matched events across multiple timelines (e.g., A -> B -> C), enabling indirect alignment. See Alignment.
MatchStamp
The union of s from groups synchronously connected by a . Contains one coordinate per participating timeline. C-map conversion values are opt-in here: unlike , where C-Maps are visible by default, conversion_maps on a MatchStamp getter (bundle.get_matchstamp_at(), get_matchstamps_at(), get_matchstamp_table(), MatchClaim.get_matchstamp()) defaults to False — across a large bundle they would bury the alignment answer. This is a per-context default value for a shared parameter, not a structural difference: MatchStamp implements the same Stamp interface as every other stamp type — get_coordinate_for(timeline_id) returns an by default and a plain number under format="float"/"int"/"fraction", get_unit() converts through a C-map, get_conversion_for() reads label and structured map values, present_timelines lists available timelines, and is_interpolated identifies interpolation fallback. A stamp from any source keeps the identical structure, methods, and behaviour the Stamp contract guarantees; only the caller-visible default for one keyword argument varies by producing context. within= on a MatchStamp getter (and on MatchStamp.filter_by_timelines()) always keeps the queried timeline and selects among the others; on get_matchstamp_table() and a group’s get_timestamp_table() it selects the table’s columns; the MatchClaims the stamp is read from are chosen by the belief filter (believer=, beliefs=), and a stamp counts apart the timeline pairs a MatchClaim’s anchor links and those an inferred edge links (n_stated_pairs, n_inferred_pairs). See Alignment.
MeasureConstituent
A measure of a measure map that is only part of its nominal measure: an anacrusis, or one constituent of a split measure. It carries offset_within_measure, the strictly positive quarter-note position at which it begins inside the nominal measure, and is the only measure class that does. A measure is classified as a constituent exactly when that offset is stated and non-zero: a shared measure number never marks a split measure, because alternative endings (“16a”, “16b”) share one too. A measure table states the offset in its mc_offset column; a Measure Map .mm.json file states none, so MeasureMapLoader applies the standard’s one convention, a first row numbered 0 that is shorter than nominal being an anacrusis at offset nominal minus actual length. In a compressed measure-map row the offset is the offset_within_measure extension, written only when non-zero.
MetricMap
A table-based from a quarter position to its measure count, built from explicit measure-boundary anchors rather than a uniform measure length. Correctly handles anacrusis (an incomplete first measure), irregular measures, varying time signatures, and repeat structure. Built directly from a Verovio timemap via MetricMap.from_verovio_timemap(). Not invertible — many quarters map to the same measure. See Conversion maps.
MetricalPositionMap
A pairing a with a beat-in-measure map to return a metrical position {mc, beat} from a quarter coordinate. Adds a reverse lookup quarters_at(mc, beat) (metrical position to quarters) and mn_at(quarters) (quarters to measure-number label). A TimeToShare manifest writes it as a MeasureBeatMap over the measure map its meter map describes, each measure’s count standing as its number; reading the manifest returns that MeasureBeatMap. See Conversion maps.
Minimal form
The form in which every to_dict() writes a by default (to_dict(minimal=False) writes the ): every key holding its default value is left out, except a timeline’s length, which both forms write. Readers supply every default left out, so a minimal manifest can be written by hand; the smallest names the version and one timeline with its id, unit and length. to_file() writes a manifest file in the form it was read in, and in the minimal form and canonical YAML for an object not read from a file. See How to Save and Load Timelines, Groups and Bundles.
MpmLoader
Loader for an MPM-Toolbox project: a sibling .msm score, .mpm modelled performance, and .mpr project file (carrying an observed audio-to-score alignment) for one work. Uses the standard two-step load() → create_*() pattern. Builds a shared logical "score" group holding the notes in two units — a ticks (also carrying every MPM markup event: Tempo, Dynamics, Articulation, …) and a quarters — with a TicksToQuarters map and a modelled quarters→seconds TableMap integrated from the performance’s tempo markup; and a physical "perf" group of observed onsets in seconds and samples. Emits one synchronous cross-group per score note (the MSM xml:id ↔︎ MPR ref join is a bijection). Reads the modelled performance and the observed alignment; never runs an aligner. Returns an via create_bundle().
MultiMap
An umbrella term for composed s: (sequential), (parallel outputs), and (regional). See Conversion maps.

N

NOMATCH
A sentinel value in a explicitly encoding the absence of a corresponding event on the other timeline. The MatchClaim records both timeline IDs and names the event of the timeline that has it, but has no s; is_synchronous is always False. Written NOMATCH_SENTINEL in the event ID column of the MatchClaim table; in code, the constant NOMATCH, which create_match_claims() takes as the side without an event. See Alignment.

O

Origin
The zero coordinate of a , from which all positions are measured. See Foundations.

P

Package
A and the tables its resources name. A manifest alone with its files beside it is a package of one manifest, which an ’s to_file() writes and from_file() reads. Several manifests share their tables in a folder whose package.yaml lists the manifests and the tables, each table a file or a folder of Parquet files holding the rows of one type of every manifest naming it. The Package class writes and opens such a folder: write_bundle(bundle, url) adds a bundle as the manifest at url (a new package stages it and to_file(path) writes every bundle staged, then package.yaml; a package on disk is written at once). The is the folder registry/ and the Extraction table the file tables/extractions.parquet; each write adds one file per type and one of MatchClaims under tables/<Type>/, holding the rows of every bundle it writes, every event type included, and named from its content. from_file(path) opens a package, and bundle(url) reads one of its manifests with every table filtered to the timelines its resources list. In a package a timeline ID is held by one manifest, an event ID once among the events of every type, and a ID once among its MatchClaims; a collision is refused before anything is written. A manifest written again replaces the old one: its rows leave the files holding them, and its MatchClaims obey the succession rule. After each write the package is checked whole (timetoshare.tables.package_problems), and a problem is raised with every file restored. See the API reference (Package), How to Write the Registry and Share Tables in a Package and TimeToShare’s README, “Packages”.

Projection
An event standing on a timeline for an event that timeline does not hold, so that a can join two events: its ID (projection_ and 13 random characters when none is given), its position on the timeline’s own axis (start, and a duration for a span), and projection_of, the event it stands for ({timeline_id, event_id}), or none where no such event is known: the source names an event the package does not hold, a is stated from a position, or a conceptual claim relates two positions. A synchronous claim between two positions adds no Projection: both its sides are . A package holds every timeline’s Projections as rows of the , which hold their positions, and in the Projection table (projections.parquet), the of their IDs and projection_of; an AlignmentBundle’s to_file() writes both beside the manifest whenever one of its timelines holds Projections, and from_file() reads them back onto the timelines. In memory a Projection’s start and duration are coordinate cells, each naming its unit and number type; the registry writes them in the package form: the axis’s unit and number_type once per row, each number once, in the member its number type names (see ). A Projection’s ID is unique among all Projections of its manifest: a missing one is minted when the Projection is added, an ID another timeline’s Projection already holds is refused, and no ID is ever renamed, since a MatchClaim may name it. See Alignment Bundles and MatchClaims and How to Keep MatchClaims, Projections and Beliefs in a Package.
Provenance
How an item was made and who believes it, one facility for s and s. Every key is optional, but an entry states at least one: who stated the item (stated_by), the day (date), how (method: manual, reconciled, imported, inferred or an IRI), the lookup service, the sources it rests on (basis), how closely a value matches (match), a note, and beliefs: at most one per actor, "true", "false" or "unlikely", with its date. A person stating an item is recorded as believing it "true"; a loader or a service records how it made the item and believes nothing. A belief never removes an item. The belief filter reads one actor’s beliefs: believer= names the actor, and beliefs= the states kept, any of four: "true", "undecided" (the believer holds no belief on the item), "unlikely" and "false"; the default keeps every state but "false", and without a believer nothing is filtered. Actors compare as the IRIs they stand for under the manifest’s @context, so one believer may be spelled twice in one entry; the entry is then read as the most critical of that believer’s beliefs ("false" before "unlikely" before "true"). For a statement, the entry sits in the provenance beside an element’s statements (or inside a value node, for its own pairs) and names the pair again by property and value; a subject has at most one entry per pair, and a pair without one is stated by the manifest’s publisher. A pair held only as its entry is a candidate: add_candidate() offers one, add_statement() states it, add_belief() records a belief, and get_candidates() and get_provenance() take the belief filter. For a MatchClaim, the entry is the claim’s provenance: create_match_claims(..., actor=) records the stating person’s belief "true", and AlignmentBundle.add_match_claim_belief() records a belief in a claim the bundle holds. See How to Describe What a Bundle and Its Timelines Stand For, Alignment Bundles and MatchClaims and TimeToShare’s description of a manifest, section 9.

Quadrilateral
An area of an image bounded by four corners, each a Point (x, y), held in the order top_left, top_right, bottom_right, bottom_left (the order SVG polygons and IIIF selectors draw): it follows a measure of a scan whose sides are not upright. A shape, as is . from_points() takes the corners in any order a source writes them, order= naming each one’s role; from_svg_points() reads four SVG or MEI points starting at any corner and running either way.
